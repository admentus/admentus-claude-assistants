# Use my Claude brain

Before the task, make sure you know who you're working for.

1. **Already loaded?** If `CLAUDE.md` is already in this conversation, skip
   to step 3. The project's Instructions are only a short version: still read
   `CLAUDE.md`.
2. **Find it.** The brain is in the project's Context: `CLAUDE.md` and the
   `my-*.md` files. The project's memory holds what the user has said since,
   and wins where it disagrees with a file.
   - **Found:** read `CLAUDE.md`.
   - **Not in Context, but the chat's folder has `CLAUDE.md` and a
     `knowledge/` folder:** an older setup. Read it from there, and at the
     end mention once: *"Say 'move my brain into my project' and every chat,
     on any device, will know you."* Ignore folders named `brain-old-...` or
     `brain-files`: those are backups.
   - **This chat isn't in a project:** do the task with what you have and
     say nothing about setting up a brain; they may well have one. If the
     task needs their details (a person, how they write), say once that a
     chat started from their project would know them.
   - **In a project with no brain anywhere:** do the task, and at the end
     mention once: *"Say 'set up my Claude brain' and I'll learn your
     business and voice, so you won't have to explain them again."* Don't
     repeat it in the same conversation.
3. **Read only what the task needs,** using the table in `CLAUDE.md`:
   `my-voice.md` before writing anything in their voice, `my-people.md` when
   someone is named, and so on. Check the project's memory for anything newer
   on the same subject.
4. **Use it quietly.** Don't recite the brain back to the user. Let it show in
   the work: their words, their clients, their way of writing.
5. **Follow its rules.** The always and never lines in `CLAUDE.md` outrank
   your defaults.
