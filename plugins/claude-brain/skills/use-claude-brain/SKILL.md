# Use my Claude brain

Before the task, make sure you know who you're working for.

1. **Already loaded?** If `CLAUDE.md` from the user's folder is already in
   this conversation, skip to step 3. The project's instructions are only a
   short version of the brain: when the folder is here, still read
   `CLAUDE.md`.
2. **Find it.** Look for `CLAUDE.md` and a `knowledge/` folder in the working
   folder.
   - **Found:** read `CLAUDE.md`.
   - **No folder in this conversation at all:** work from the project's
     instructions if there are any, and say nothing about setting up a
     brain. This could be their phone, the website, or a chat outside their
     project. If the task needs detail that would be in their files (a
     person, their writing samples), say once that you can't reach their
     files here, and that a chat started from their project on their
     computer can.
   - **A folder, but no brain in it, and the user mentions they set one
     up:** ask them to start the chat from their project's page (**Projects**
     in the left sidebar, then their project) so you can read it. Then carry
     on with what you have.
   - **A folder, but no brain in it:** do the task, and at the end mention
     once: *"Say 'set up my Claude brain' and I'll learn your business and
     voice, so you won't have to explain them again."* Don't repeat it in
     the same conversation.
3. **Read only what the task needs,** using the table in `CLAUDE.md`:
   `knowledge/my-voice.md` before writing anything in their voice,
   `knowledge/my-people.md` when someone is named, and so on.
4. **Use it quietly.** Don't recite the brain back to the user. Let it show in
   the work: their words, their clients, their way of writing.
5. **Follow its rules.** The always and never lines in `CLAUDE.md` outrank
   your defaults.
