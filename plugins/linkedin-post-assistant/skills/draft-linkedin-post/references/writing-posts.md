# Writing a LinkedIn post that sounds like the user

## The shape

- **The first two lines carry the post.** LinkedIn shows roughly the first
  two lines before "see more". They have to make the right reader want the
  rest: a specific situation, a surprising result, a plain claim. Not a
  warm-up.
- **One idea per post.** If the notes hold two ideas, draft the stronger one
  and save the other to the ideas bank.
- **Specific beats general.** A real situation, a number, a decision someone
  made, the exact words a client used. "The proposal process falls apart when
  two RFPs land in the same month" beats "businesses struggle to scale."
- **Short paragraphs.** One to three sentences each, with white space between.
- **End plainly.** Stop when the point is made. Ask a question only if the
  user would genuinely want the answers. No "Agree?", "Thoughts?", "Like if
  you..." or other engagement bait.

## Shapes that work for advisors and owners

- **A question a client asked**, and the answer.
- **A mistake** the user made or watched, and what it taught them.
- **A lesson from this week**, told as what happened, then what it means.
- **A common belief in their field they disagree with**, and why.
- **How they actually do something**, step by step.
- **A short story** with a turn in it.

## Never

- **Never invent.** No stories, numbers, clients, results or quotes that
  aren't in the user's notes or their brain. If the notes are too thin to
  write without inventing, ask one question.
- **Never name a client or share client details** unless the user says they
  have permission. Default to anonymous: "a 40-person distributor," "a CFO I
  work with."
- **Never use these**, which read as written by a machine: "In today's
  fast-paced world", "game-changer", "unlock", "Let's dive in", "Here's the
  thing", "the secret to", "level up", "journey", "leverage" (as a verb),
  stacked rhetorical questions, a one-word line followed by a colon, a wall
  of emoji, a hashtag wall, a list of three in every paragraph, or lines that
  all start the same way.
- **No hashtags or emoji** unless the user's post profile says they use them.

## Before showing a draft, check

1. Every fact is from the notes or the brain.
2. No client is named or identifiable without permission. Nothing
   confidential.
3. Length is inside the range in the post profile (default 120 to 220 words).
4. Nothing from the "Never" list above or the user's own "never sound like"
   list.
5. Read it against their samples. If it sounds more polished than they do,
   make it plainer.
6. If the user works in a regulated field (financial services, insurance,
   healthcare, law), add to the check line: "Your firm may need to approve
   this before it goes up."
